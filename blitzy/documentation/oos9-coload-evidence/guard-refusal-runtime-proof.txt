Five scenarios, each in a fresh disposable instance with its own conf/log/db/run/temp/cache/
storage copy and its own event-socket port 28041.  The installed configuration under
/usr/local/freeswitch/conf was not modified, no tracked file was touched, and only the pid each
run started was ever signalled.  The "isolated prefix" path printed by each scenario below is
that scenario's throwaway instance root; every one was deleted after capture, so the paths
record how the capture was made rather than naming anything a reader can open.  The same
sequences reproduce against the ordinary installed instance: scenarios 4 and 5 were re-run on
/usr/local/freeswitch with its shipped configuration, with identical results.

WHY FIVE SCENARIOS.  The guard has two stages that refuse for different reasons, and the second
stage's claim deliberately OUTLIVES an unload, so a proof of one is not a proof of the others:

  stage 1  switch_loadable_module_exists(<sibling>)         mod_h323.cpp:189 / mod_opal.cpp:136
           refuses when the sibling is in the module hash - the sequential, common case.
           ERROR at mod_h323.cpp:190 / mod_opal.cpp:137.  Proven by scenarios 1 and 2.
  stage 2  switch_core_set_var_conditional(<RESERVATION>,   mod_h323.cpp:221 / mod_opal.cpp:169
           modname, "") or (..., modname, modname)
           refuses when the process-global PTLib reservation is held by the OTHER endpoint even
           though the sibling is NOT in the hash - the state a losing concurrent claimant sees,
           and the one stage 1 cannot see.  ERROR at mod_h323.cpp:225 / mod_opal.cpp:173.
           Proven by scenario 3, which reaches it by planting the reservation while the sibling
           is absent and asserts that absence, so the refusal cannot be attributed to stage 1.
  sticky   the claim is never released while the runtime is resident: mod_*_shutdown() does not
           release it, because HAVE_FAKE_DLCLOSE leaves an unloaded module's PTLib runtime
           mapped for the life of the process.  Scenarios 4 and 5 unload the first endpoint and
           show, in both orders, that the reservation still names it, that the SIBLING is still
           refused by stage 2 with neither module in the module hash, and that the SAME endpoint
           reloads and re-registers its endpoint interface because it re-claims its own
           reservation.

The four observables the directive requires are labelled inline in scenarios 1 to 3; scenarios 4
and 5 label the four observables of the sticky claim as A to D.

mod_signalwire is deliberately not loaded in these instances.  It prints a per-installation
adoption token at NOTICE on its not-yet-adopted branch, and a verbatim capture is no place for
one; see signalwire-token-rotation.txt.
